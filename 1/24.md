## Idea

### Title

**LFCC (Lattice FPGA Companion Chip)** — a collection of ready-to-use projects and bitstreams that convert Lattice flash FPGAs into companion chips, providing common interface functions (autobaudrate UART, I2C, SPI, ULi, and others) without requiring users to write any HDL. Two design variants are offered: simple LFCC for minimum-cost deployment and smart LFCC with embedded RISC-V BASIC programmability.

### Description

LFCC is a concept for productising Lattice flash FPGAs as companion chips. The deliverable is a collection of pre-built bitstreams and source projects that engineers can use directly, without running FPGA tooling and without writing any HDL.

**The companion chip concept:**

In embedded systems, a companion chip is a small auxiliary IC that handles specific tasks alongside a main processor. Companion chips typically provide interface bridging, protocol translation, specialised I/O, glue logic, or expanded peripheral capabilities. Fixed-function companion chip ICs are widely used in embedded designs — UART bridges, I2C-to-SPI converters, GPIO expanders, protocol translators, and others. Each fixed-function chip serves a specific need.

LFCC proposes that a Lattice flash FPGA can replace many of these fixed-function chips through pre-built bitstreams. The user selects the appropriate bitstream for the interfaces they need, loads it into a Lattice flash FPGA, and has a working companion chip. The FPGA serves as a configurable replacement for fixed-function companion chip ICs, with the configuration determined by which bitstream is loaded.

**Two LFCC design variants:**

LFCC is offered in two variants that target different cost and capability points.

**Simple LFCC.** The smaller variant fits in the smallest available Lattice LFMXO4 device, providing a minimum-cost companion chip configuration. The simple variant focuses on essential interface bridging and protocol translation functions. The initial simple LFCC configuration provides ULi support, giving engineers immediate access to single-wire bidirectional communication in the smallest possible footprint and at the lowest possible cost. Additional simple LFCC configurations may provide other single-interface or minimal multi-interface combinations as the catalogue develops.

**Smart LFCC.** The larger variant fits in LFMXO4-025 and provides programmable companion chip functionality through embedded BASIC support. Smart LFCC includes either RBC (RISC-V Basic Compiler, registered separately in this registry on 2026-05-15) or RBI (RISC-V Basic Interpreter, also registered separately on 2026-05-15), enabling engineers to customise companion chip behaviour through BASIC programming rather than through HDL modification. This makes smart LFCC suitable for applications where the companion chip needs to be tailored to specific protocol behaviours, where interface behaviour needs to evolve after deployment, or where users want the flexibility of programmable companion chip functionality without the complexity of FPGA development.

The two variants serve different markets. Simple LFCC competes directly with fixed-function companion chip ICs on cost and footprint. Smart LFCC competes with more sophisticated programmable peripheral ICs on flexibility, while remaining significantly less expensive than dedicated programmable peripherals.

**Initial interface support:**

LFCC bitstreams provide the following interfaces:

- **Autobaudrate UART.** A UART interface that automatically detects the baud rate of incoming serial data, removing the need for explicit baud rate configuration. Particularly useful when the deployment scenario does not know the host's baud rate in advance.
- **I2C.** Two-wire serial bus interface, supporting master, slave, or combined operation depending on the specific bitstream configuration.
- **SPI.** Serial peripheral interface bus, supporting master, slave, or combined operation depending on the specific bitstream configuration.
- **ULi (Universal Link).** Single-wire bidirectional clock-synchronous protocol, registered separately in this registry on 2026-05-07. LFCC bitstreams enable ULi communication on Lattice FPGAs as a companion chip function, giving LFCC users immediate access to single-wire bidirectional connectivity. ULi is included in the simple LFCC variant as its initial interface, providing minimum-cost ULi deployment.

Additional interfaces may be added to the LFCC catalogue over time. Candidates include CAN, USB device, parallel buses, custom or domain-specific protocols, and combinations of multiple interfaces in a single bitstream.

**The two deliverable layers:**

LFCC has two layers of deliverable that serve different user needs.

**The bitstreams** are the immediately usable artefacts. Engineers who want a companion chip download the bitstream that provides the interfaces they need, load it into a Lattice flash FPGA, and use the resulting chip in their design. No HDL is written. No FPGA tooling is run. The bitstream is the product.

**The source projects** are the FPGA design files (HDL source, constraints, project configuration) that produced each bitstream. Engineers who want to customise LFCC behaviour beyond the pre-built configurations can open the source projects in Lattice Radiant, modify the design, and generate their own customised bitstreams. The source projects are useful for engineers who need interface combinations or behaviours that the pre-built bitstreams do not cover.

### Why Lattice flash FPGAs specifically

Lattice flash FPGAs are particularly well-suited to the companion chip role for several specific reasons.

**Internal flash configuration.** Lattice MachXO2, MachXO3, MachXO5, LFMXO4, and similar parts include internal flash memory that holds the configuration bitstream. The FPGA does not need external configuration memory. From the perspective of the rest of the system, the LFCC-equipped Lattice part behaves like a fixed-function IC — power it on, it works, no visible boot sequence.

**Instant-on configuration.** Lattice flash FPGAs are typically ready to function within microseconds of power-up, since the configuration is loaded from internal flash automatically. This matches the behaviour expected of companion chip ICs and matters for applications where the main processor needs to communicate with the companion chip immediately at power-up.

**Small package availability.** Lattice flash FPGAs are available in package sizes appropriate for board space constraints typical of companion chip applications — QFN, BGA, and other small packages.

**Cost competitiveness.** Lattice flash FPGAs are priced competitively with dedicated companion chip ICs. An LFCC-equipped Lattice part offers comparable cost while providing reconfigurability that fixed-function ICs cannot match. The smallest LFMXO4 devices are particularly cost-competitive for simple LFCC deployments.

**The LFMXO4 product line specifically.** The Lattice LFMXO4 family provides a range of capacity points suitable for both LFCC variants. The smallest LFMXO4 device is appropriate for simple LFCC deployments where minimum cost is essential. The LFMXO4-025 provides sufficient capacity for smart LFCC with embedded BASIC programmability while remaining cost-competitive with sophisticated companion chip ICs from other vendors.

**Established ecosystem.** Lattice has invested substantially in tooling, documentation, and community support for their flash FPGA product lines. Engineers working with LFCC have the supporting infrastructure to make the work practical.

### Why this idea matters

The conventional companion chip market is fragmented across many vendors, each offering specific fixed-function ICs for specific interface needs. Engineers designing embedded systems often need to select multiple companion chips from different vendors, each with its own datasheet, footprint, and supply chain. The result is design complexity, BOM management overhead, and supply chain risk distributed across many parts.

LFCC addresses this by providing a unified family of companion chip functions implemented on a single FPGA part family. Instead of stocking multiple fixed-function ICs, engineers stock one or two Lattice flash FPGA variants and load the appropriate bitstream for each use case. Multiple companion chip functions in a single design can be implemented through a single LFCC-equipped Lattice part with a combined bitstream that provides all the needed interfaces simultaneously.

The two-variant structure serves the market more effectively than a single-variant approach would. Simple LFCC addresses cost-sensitive applications where fixed-function ICs would otherwise be selected. Smart LFCC addresses applications where programmable customisation provides value beyond what fixed-function alternatives can offer. Together the two variants cover the spectrum from minimum-cost companion chip replacement to flexible programmable peripheral functionality.

Specific application domains that benefit:

- **Cost-sensitive embedded designs** where reducing BOM complexity matters. Simple LFCC replaces multiple companion chip ICs at competitive cost.
- **Legacy system retrofits** where engineers need to bridge old protocols to new systems. LFCC bitstreams with appropriate interface combinations can serve as protocol translators. Smart LFCC with RBI provides interactive customisation of bridging behaviour through on-target BASIC editing.
- **Prototyping and small-volume production** where flexibility matters more than per-unit cost optimisation. Smart LFCC particularly suits this use case because the BASIC programmability allows the companion chip behaviour to evolve during development without changing hardware.
- **Educational embedded computing** where students can experiment with different interfaces using the same physical hardware by loading different LFCC bitstreams. Smart LFCC with RBI provides a Basic-Stamp-style educational experience using contemporary Lattice FPGA hardware.
- **ULi adoption** specifically. Simple LFCC provides ULi support out of the box on Lattice FPGAs at the minimum possible cost and footprint, lowering the barrier to using ULi in real designs and providing the first downstream productisation of the ULi protocol.

### Domain or field

FPGA productisation, embedded systems, companion chip ICs, interface bridging, Lattice FPGA ecosystem, ready-to-use FPGA design distribution, programmable peripherals.

### References to related ideas

- **Fixed-function companion chip ICs** from various vendors — FTDI USB bridges, Maxim/Analog Devices interface ICs, Microchip GPIO expanders, NXP I2C/SPI bridges, and many others. The conventional market that simple LFCC competes with by offering reconfigurable equivalents on Lattice FPGAs.
- **Programmable peripheral ICs** such as Parallax Propeller, Cypress PSoC, various microcontrollers with FPGA-like configurable peripherals. The market that smart LFCC competes with by offering BASIC-based programmability on Lattice FPGAs.
- **MicroFPGA** (registered in this registry on 2026-05-08). The conceptually related project that makes FPGAs programmable in C without HDL. MicroFPGA and smart LFCC are closely related — both use embedded processors with pre-built bitstreams to make FPGAs accessible to non-HDL developers. The two concepts differ in their target user: MicroFPGA serves engineers who want C programming with many GPIO pins; smart LFCC serves engineers who want a programmable companion chip with specific interface support.
- **ULi (Universal Link)** registered in this registry on 2026-05-07. The single-wire bidirectional protocol that simple LFCC provides as its initial interface. LFCC gives ULi its first downstream productisation.
- **ASIO (A Simple I/O)** registered in this registry on 2026-05-08. The memory-mapped GPIO IP core that may be used internally in smart LFCC designs where the embedded BASIC code needs to control GPIO pins.
- **RBC (RISC-V Basic Compiler)** registered in this registry on 2026-05-15. The cross-compiler that smart LFCC may use for BASIC programmability when compiled execution is preferred.
- **RBI (RISC-V Basic Interpreter)** registered in this registry on 2026-05-15. The on-target interpreter that smart LFCC may use for BASIC programmability when interactive on-target development is preferred.
- **Basic Stamp from Parallax.** The historical example of companion-chip-style programmable BASIC devices. Smart LFCC with RBI provides similar functionality on contemporary Lattice FPGA hardware.
- **Various open-source FPGA design distributions.** OpenCores, F4PGA, and similar projects that distribute FPGA designs as open source. LFCC follows the same general pattern of distributing usable FPGA designs to engineers who can use them directly or customise them.

The specific contribution of LFCC is the productised collection of ready-to-use Lattice FPGA bitstreams and source projects that turn Lattice flash FPGAs into companion chips, with the two-variant structure (simple for minimum cost, smart for programmability) that serves different segments of the companion chip market.

## Remarks

This registration documents LFCC as a productisation concept that combines several elements: Lattice flash FPGAs as the hardware substrate, pre-built bitstreams as the deployment mechanism, a two-variant structure that serves different market segments, common interfaces (UART, I2C, SPI, ULi) as the initial scope, and BASIC programmability through RBC or RBI in the smart variant.

**On the two-variant structure.** The simple/smart split allows LFCC to address both ends of the companion chip market simultaneously. Simple LFCC competes on cost; smart LFCC competes on capability. Engineers select the variant that fits their specific application, with the option to move from simple to smart as their needs evolve. The two variants share the same overall framework — Lattice flash FPGA, pre-built bitstreams, ready-to-use deployment — while serving different specific needs.

**On the LFMXO4-025 capacity point.** The choice of LFMXO4-025 for smart LFCC reflects the capacity needed to support RBC or RBI alongside the companion chip interface functions. The LFMXO4-025 provides enough fabric for the embedded RISC-V softcore, the BASIC compiler or interpreter, the interface implementations, and the supporting infrastructure. Smaller LFMXO4 devices may not provide sufficient capacity for the full smart LFCC configuration, which is why the simple LFCC variant exists for cost-sensitive deployments that do not need the programmable capability.

**On the ULi inclusion in simple LFCC specifically.** Including ULi as the simple LFCC variant's initial interface serves two purposes. It gives simple LFCC users a useful protocol at minimum cost. It also gives ULi its lowest-cost downstream deployment path, making ULi accessible to engineers who want single-wire bidirectional communication without committing to larger Lattice parts or to FPGA development effort. The combination — simple LFCC with ULi on the smallest LFMXO4 — represents the minimum viable ULi adoption configuration.

**On the relationship to MicroFPGA.** Smart LFCC and MicroFPGA share underlying technology (pre-built bitstreams with embedded processors enabling non-HDL programming on Lattice FPGAs). They differ in user focus: MicroFPGA targets engineers wanting C-programmable platforms with many GPIO pins; smart LFCC targets engineers wanting companion chip functionality with BASIC programmability. The two concepts could share implementation infrastructure even though they serve different audiences. A unified product family encompassing both could reuse the embedded processor, the bitstream distribution mechanism, and the documentation framework.

**On the relationship to RBC and RBI.** Smart LFCC depends on RBC or RBI to provide the BASIC programmability that distinguishes it from simple LFCC. The choice between RBC (compiled BASIC) and RBI (interpreted BASIC) for smart LFCC implementations depends on the specific application's needs. RBC-based smart LFCC provides maximum runtime performance and minimum program memory footprint. RBI-based smart LFCC provides interactive on-target development with UART editor support. Both options may be offered as separate smart LFCC configurations within the broader LFCC catalogue.

**On the implementation roadmap.** This registration documents the LFCC concept. The actual implementation — the pre-built bitstreams for simple and smart LFCC on specific Lattice parts, the source projects, the documentation, the distribution mechanism, the integration with RBC or RBI — is future work. Whether LFCC gets implemented by the registrant, by Lattice Semiconductor, by someone else who picks up the idea, or never at all is open. The registration establishes the public record of the concept and makes it available under MIT license for any developer to implement.
