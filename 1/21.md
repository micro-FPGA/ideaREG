## Idea

### Title

**LMC (Lattice Motuper Contest)** — a proposed open-ended engineering contest sponsored by Lattice Semiconductor, with a Grand Prize awarded to the world's first working motuper and annual creativity prizes for submitted projects that do not qualify for the Grand Prize.

### Description

LMC is a proposed engineering contest with an unusual structure. The contest would have no fixed deadline. The Grand Prize would remain open until a specific outcome is achieved: the world's first motuper gets invented. Smaller annual creativity prizes would be awarded each year to creative projects submitted that do not qualify for the Grand Prize, allowing the contest to produce useful work even if the Grand Prize is never claimed.

**Contest structure:**

The Grand Prize is the contest's primary objective. It would be awarded to the world's first working motuper. The MOTUPER concept (registered separately in this registry on 2026-05-15) is currently a name reserved for a hypothetical hardware architecture that combines elements of the Transputer and the Computer to do some sort of computing in some new way. The specific nature of the new way has not been determined. The Grand Prize would be claimed by whoever first produces a working architecture that the engineering community recognises as a motuper.

The annual creativity prizes are the contest's secondary reward structure. Submitted projects that demonstrate creative engineering work but do not qualify as motupers would be eligible. The prizes would be awarded annually based on submissions received in each year, allowing the contest to produce a continuous stream of recognised work rather than being entirely dependent on the Grand Prize being claimed.

**Submission requirements:**

- The motuper architecture must be scalable, with smaller configurations capable of running on Lattice FPGAs.
- Submissions must include a full Radiant project (Radiant being Lattice Semiconductor's FPGA design toolchain), allowing the submitted work to be verified and reproduced on Lattice hardware.

**Sponsorship and offers:**

The contest proposal is presented to Lattice Semiconductor as a potential sponsorship opportunity. The registrant offers to transfer the motuper.com and truedream.org domains to Lattice Semiconductor if the contest is established, providing the contest with established intellectual-property foundations for its public presence. The registrant has also offered to finance the prize money personally if Lattice does not wish to fund the prizes from corporate funds.

### Why the unusual structure makes sense

The conventional engineering contest has a fixed deadline, a defined set of evaluation criteria, and a single prize awarded once. This structure works well for problems with clear specifications and predictable solution paths. It works poorly for problems where the outcome is genuinely novel and may take an indefinite period to achieve.

The motuper problem is exactly the kind of problem the conventional structure does not serve well. No one currently knows what a motuper is. The concept may turn out to be impossible. The concept may take decades to be articulated, let alone implemented. The concept may be invented by someone who is not currently working in FPGA design at all.

The LMC structure addresses this. The Grand Prize remains open indefinitely, accommodating any timeline for the eventual invention. The annual creativity prizes ensure that the contest produces value even if the Grand Prize is never claimed — engineers who submit interesting projects that fall short of the Grand Prize get recognition and modest financial reward for their work. The contest becomes a long-term framework for creative FPGA engineering rather than a one-time event.

The SERV precedent supports this structure. SERV, the bit-serial RISC-V softcore by Olof Kindgren, won only the creativity prize in the FPGA contest where Antti Lukats's Engine-V won first prize. SERV later evolved into a genuinely useful product that the broader FPGA community has adopted. The Grand Prize was not the contest's only valuable output. The creativity prize for SERV recognised work that would prove substantial over time. The LMC structure formalises this pattern — creativity prizes are not consolation rewards but recognition of work that may turn out to matter.

### Why Lattice Semiconductor specifically

The contest is proposed specifically for Lattice Semiconductor for several reasons.

**FPGA architecture fit.** Lattice FPGAs are well-suited to the scalable-with-smaller-configurations requirement. The Lattice product line includes parts at multiple capacity tiers, allowing motuper concepts to be tested at small scales on inexpensive devices before scaling up. The Lattice ecosystem supports the *let many people try this* model that the LMC depends on.

**Open-source culture.** Lattice has been notably supportive of open-source FPGA tooling (Project Trellis, IceStorm, the broader open-source toolchain ecosystem for Lattice parts). The LMC's emphasis on submissions that include full Radiant projects allows verification while still being accessible to engineers working in the open-source spirit.

**Strategic positioning.** A long-running contest associated with Lattice would give the company a distinctive marketing presence in the FPGA community. The motuper concept's open-ended character means that the contest would generate ongoing engineering interest and submissions over many years, providing Lattice with continuous community engagement that one-time contests do not produce.

**The registrant's relationship with Lattice's ecosystem.** The registrant has been working with Lattice FPGAs across his career, has contributed FPGA-related work to the broader community including the FPGA Bible reference work in preparation, and has the relationships with Lattice's existing customer base that would help the contest gain initial submissions.

### Domain or field

Engineering contests, FPGA community engagement, novel computing architecture, open-ended challenge structures, the MOTUPER concept.

### References to related ideas

- **MOTUPER** (registered in this registry on 2026-05-15). The hypothetical hardware concept that the LMC's Grand Prize is designed to reward.
- **The FPGA contest where Engine-V won second prize and SERV won creativity prize.** The historical precedent that establishes the value of creativity prizes alongside grand prizes. Both projects went on to have substantial impact in the FPGA community.

Existing examples of open-ended challenge contests with grand prizes that may take indefinite time to claim. The LMC structure draws on this tradition.

The specific contribution of LMC is the contest structure that combines an indefinitely-open Grand Prize with annual creativity prizes, applied to the motuper problem with Lattice Semiconductor as the proposed sponsor.

## Remarks

This registration documents the LMC proposal as articulated in the appendix to a letter sent on 2026-05-15 to Ford Tamer, CEO of Lattice Semiconductor. The proposal is being put forward to Lattice for their consideration. Whether Lattice will adopt the proposal is a matter for Lattice's decision; the registration here documents the proposal as it stands regardless of the outcome.

**On the contest sponsor question.** The proposal names Lattice Semiconductor specifically as the proposed sponsor. If Lattice declines or does not respond, the LMC concept is not therefore dead. Other FPGA vendors could sponsor a similar contest under different names. Other organisations interested in novel computing architectures could sponsor the contest themselves. The MIT license under which this registration is made allows anyone to take the LMC concept and apply it in any context, with attribution to the registrant preserved.

**On the registrant's personal financial commitment.** The registrant has stated, in the Antti Bible's appendix, that if his son brought him the LMC idea he would finance the prize money from his private pocket. The statement is preserved as part of the proposal's character. If Lattice declines to fund the prize money but allows the contest to proceed under their name, the registrant's offer to fund the prizes personally remains on the table. If Lattice declines entirely, the registrant retains the option to establish the contest himself under a different name with personal funding.

**On the broader framework.** The LMC is one example of a structural pattern that may be useful in other contexts. The combination of an indefinitely-open grand prize with annual creativity prizes addresses a specific kind of problem — problems where the eventual outcome is genuinely uncertain and the timeline is indefinite. The pattern may be applicable to other problems beyond MOTUPER. The registration here documents the pattern as it applies to LMC specifically; applications to other problems would be separate registrations.

The LMC registration is connected to the MOTUPER registration (also registered on 2026-05-15) in the same registry. The MOTUPER registration documents the name and the hypothetical architecture concept. The LMC registration documents the proposed contest structure that would reward the invention of a motuper. Together the two registrations establish the public-record foundation for the contest, whether or not Lattice adopts it as the sponsor.
