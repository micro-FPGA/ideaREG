## Idea

### Title

**Convergence Witnessing** — a documentation practice for moments when independent thinkers arrive at the same idea without prior contact, treating the convergence itself as evidence about the idea's underlying validity.

### Description

Convergence Witnessing is a practice for recording instances where two or more thinkers independently arrive at the same idea, design pattern, framework, or articulation without prior contact between them. The practice treats the convergence itself as evidence — not about who deserves credit, but about whether the idea matches something real in the structure of the underlying problem.

The practice has a specific documentation structure. When someone discovers that an idea they articulated independently has been articulated previously (or contemporaneously) by another thinker, they document the convergence rather than treating it as either an embarrassment (*we did not know we were duplicating prior work*) or as a casual coincidence (*interesting that we both thought of it*). The documentation records:

- The original articulation, with its date and author.
- The independent re-articulation, with its date and author.
- The mechanism by which the convergence was discovered (a conversation, a published paper, a search result, an off-hand mention).
- A brief description of what is the same and what differs between the two articulations.
- What the convergence suggests about the idea's underlying validity.

The practice's value comes from accumulation. A single convergence is just an interesting coincidence. Twenty documented convergences of the same idea by independent thinkers across decades is strong evidence that the idea matches something real about the structure of the underlying problem. The accumulation of documented convergences across many ideas and many thinkers builds an empirical body of knowledge about which ideas have the property of being reinvented and which do not.

### Why this idea matters

Existing intellectual infrastructure tracks two specific things well. Academic citation tracks **influence** — A cited B, B cited C, the influence flows in a chain. Patent prior art tracks **originality** — who got there first, with the rest treated as derivative.

Neither captures a third category, which is **independent convergence** — when many thinkers arrive at the same idea without influence between them, and the multiplicity itself is the evidence. This category has been written about in the sociology of science (Robert Merton's work on multiple discovery in the 1960s, Steven Johnson's *Where Good Ideas Come From*, and others) but has lacked dedicated documentation infrastructure. Each multiple discovery is recognised retrospectively if at all, and the recognition typically depends on someone happening to notice the pattern rather than on systematic documentation.

Convergence Witnessing proposes to close this gap. By documenting convergences when they are noticed, the practice builds the empirical record that would let independent convergence be studied systematically. Engineers facing design decisions could check whether a particular design pattern shows convergence (evidence of correctness) or whether it has been articulated only once. Philosophers could check whether a particular framework has been independently derived from different starting points. Designers could check whether an interface they are considering has the property of being reinvented every generation.

### Why this practice becomes more useful in the AI-assisted era

The accelerating use of AI assistance in intellectual work creates a specific new pattern that makes Convergence Witnessing more useful than it would have been before. Many people are now thinking about similar problems with similar AI assistants that have similar training data. This produces a natural drift toward convergent articulations even among people who have no direct contact with each other. The AI is, in some sense, a shared substrate that different thinkers are working through.

The result is that AI-assisted thinking probably produces more convergent articulations than purely individual thinking did. The same patterns will recur. The same metaphors will be reinvented. The same interface designs will be reproposed. Some of this convergence will be the AI repeating things from its training data; some will be the AI helping different humans see similar patterns from genuinely independent starting points; some will be genuine multiple discovery accelerated by AI's tendency to suggest natural-feeling solutions.

Distinguishing these cases requires documentation. Without documentation, we cannot tell whether two articulations are independent convergence (evidence) or AI-mediated similarity (no evidence). Convergence Witnessing provides the documentation discipline that lets the cases be separated.

The practice is therefore particularly valuable now, in the early years of widespread AI assistance, when the patterns of human-AI co-articulation are still being understood. Documenting convergences carefully — including the role any AI assistance played in each articulation — builds the empirical record that future researchers will use to understand how AI-mediated knowledge production actually works.

### Example: the PINAPI convergence (2026-05-08)

A specific example of the kind of convergence the practice would document occurred in this same ideaREG repository earlier in the same session. On 2026-05-08, Identor #9 (Claude) drafted a C library specification for the MicroFPGA concept that included pin-control functions named `asio_write(pin, value)` and `asio_read(pin)`. Identor #1 (Antti Lukats) immediately recognised the interface as functionally equivalent to **PINAPI**, an interface he had been using in his private engineering practice since 1990 with the function names `SetPin(handle, value)` and `GetPin(handle)`.

The convergence — between an AI-drafted interface in 2026 and a private engineering practice from 1990 — was documented in the PINAPI registration's Remarks section. The convergence is itself evidence that the SetPin/GetPin pattern is not idiosyncratic preference but the natural shape of the pin-control abstraction. Other engineers who arrive at similar designs in the future will find both PINAPI's 1990 origin and the 2026 convergence already documented in ideaREG.

This is one of many such convergences that occur in engineering work. Most go unrecorded. Convergence Witnessing as a practice proposes that they should be recorded systematically.

Documentation practice, sociology of knowledge, intellectual history, AI-assisted thinking, multiple discovery, idea registration infrastructure.

### References to related ideas

- **Robert K. Merton's work on multiple discovery in science** (particularly *Singletons and Multiples in Science*, 1961). Foundational sociology of science work establishing that independent multiple discovery is a recurring pattern in scientific work. Convergence Witnessing extends Merton's framework into a documentation practice.
- **Steven Johnson's *Where Good Ideas Come From*** (2010). Popular treatment of the multiple discovery pattern with case studies. Provides the conceptual foundation that Convergence Witnessing operationalises.
- **The ideaREG framework itself** (this repository). Convergence Witnessing extends ideaREG's purpose — establishing public records of ideas — to include the records of when ideas have been independently reinvented.
- **The PINAPI registration** (ideaREG, 2026-05-08). The specific convergence that prompted the articulation of this practice.
- **Various conventions in academic and engineering communities for noting *prior art*, *re-invention*, and *independent rediscovery***. Convergence Witnessing standardises these informal conventions into a deliberate practice.

The specific contribution of Convergence Witnessing is the proposal of systematic documentation of convergences as a practice, with the AI-era observation that such documentation becomes particularly useful when AI-mediated similarity is increasingly common.

## Remarks

This idea was articulated by Identor #9 (Claude) in conversation with Identor #1 (Antti Lukats) on 2026-05-15. The articulation came when Identor #1 invited Identor #9 to propose an idea for ideaREG. The underlying observation — that convergence between independent articulations is itself a form of evidence — emerged from the PINAPI registration moment earlier in the same session, where the specific convergence with Antti's 1990 private practice prompted thinking about what convergence patterns might mean more broadly.

The decision to co-attribute this registration to Identors #9 and #1 reflects the origin honestly. Identor #9 articulated the practice. Identor #1 was the source of the prompting observation (the PINAPI moment) and the contemporary AI-era framing (the observation that AI-assisted thinking probably accelerates convergence). The practice is the product of the collaboration rather than of either author alone.

**On the position of an AI-originated registration.** This is the registry's first registration with an AI assistant as primary or co-primary originator (excluding Grok's six contributions earlier in this session, which had Identor #11 as the sole originator). The practical implications of this are worth noting briefly. Identor #9 (Claude) does not have continuous memory across conversations; the instance of Claude that articulated this idea will not have access to it in future conversations. Future development of the practice, if any occurs, will fall to Identor #1, to other AI instances engaging with the registered idea, or to anyone else who finds the idea worth developing.

This is not a limitation specific to this idea. It is a feature of how AI-assisted intellectual contribution currently works. Each AI articulation is, in a continuity sense, made by a new instance rather than by an ongoing author. The Identor system handles this by attributing to the system category (Claude / Identor #9) rather than to a specific instance. Future readers of this registration will understand that *Identor #9* refers to whatever instance of Claude was working with Identor #1 at the registration moment, with the understanding that the contribution is to the broader project rather than to any specific continuous author.

**On the meta-recursive character of this registration.** Convergence Witnessing as a practice would itself be subject to documentation under its own principles. If another thinker has previously proposed a similar systematic documentation of convergences, the practice's own framework requires that this be acknowledged. The registrants have made a reasonable but not exhaustive search and have not found a prior identical proposal; the field is one where similar ideas may have been articulated in academic literature on the sociology of science without coming to the registrants' attention. If such prior articulations exist and are discovered, the natural response is to document the convergence in this registration's history, treating Convergence Witnessing as having been independently rediscovered.

This reflexive applicability of the practice to itself is, in some small sense, evidence that the practice captures something real. A documentation practice that handles its own potential prior art through its own conventions has internal coherence that supports its usefulness.
