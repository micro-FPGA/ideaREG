## Idea

### Title

**MOTUPER** — a name reserved for a hypothetical computing hardware concept that combines elements of the Transputer and the Computer, intended to do some sort of computing in some new way. The specific nature of the new way has not been determined.

### Description

MOTUPER is a name. The name was coined by combining *TRANSPUTER* and *COMPUTER*. The Transputer was a 1980s parallel-computing microprocessor architecture from Inmos that arranged multiple processors with built-in communication channels, allowing concurrent computation across many cores connected by serial links. The Computer is the universal name for general-purpose programmable digital machines that has been the dominant computing paradigm since the mid-twentieth century.

MOTUPER as a concept is the suggestion that some hardware architecture might combine elements of these two traditions to do computing in some new way. What that new way is has not been specified. The registrant has admitted, in the *Dreams* entry of the Antti Bible, that he does not know how MOTUPER may operate and does not think he ever will.

The name MOTUPER and the domain **motuper.com** exist as a reserved space for a future concept that may or may not be developed. The registration here documents the name and the gesture toward an architecture, without committing to any specific technical claims about what the architecture would actually be.

### Why register a concept this incomplete

The registration captures a specific kind of intellectual contribution that conventional intellectual-property frameworks do not handle well. A name with a vague gesture toward an idea is not patentable. It is not copyrightable as an idea. It is not registrable as a trademark unless attached to a specific product. But it is something — a piece of intellectual territory that the registrant has marked with a domain purchase and that has accumulated a small associative weight over the years.

The Bible's broader framework recognises ideas at all stages of development, including stages where the idea is genuinely incomplete. ULi was a complete protocol design that waited ten years before being implemented. ASIO was a clear design that reached working implementation in one day. PINAPI was a long-running engineering practice that had never been publicly registered. MOTUPER is at the other end of this spectrum — a name reserved for an idea that has not yet been articulated even in concept, and that may never be.

Registering MOTUPER here serves a few specific purposes. It documents the name's existence and the registrant's claim on it. It captures the date of registration so any future development can be placed in time relative to this moment. It makes the name available to anyone — including the registrant himself in some future moment, or anyone else — who may want to develop a real concept that the name fits.

The MIT license under which this registration is made means that anyone is free to develop a MOTUPER concept and to use the name. If someone produces a real MOTUPER architecture in the future, the name is available to them. The registration does not block development; it merely documents the name's existing public claim.

Computing hardware architecture, name reservation, hypothetical engineering concepts, Transputer descendants, novel computing paradigms.

### References to related ideas

- **The Transputer** (Inmos, 1980s). A real parallel-computing architecture with built-in communication channels between processors. The first half of the MOTUPER name comes from this lineage. Transputer-derived architectures still influence parallel computing design.
- **General-purpose computing.** The second half of the MOTUPER name comes from the universal *Computer* category. The combination suggests a hybrid architecture that draws on both traditions.
- **The Antti Bible's *Dreams* entry.** The entry where MOTUPER is mentioned, where the registrant admits he does not know how it would operate and does not think he ever will. The entry's honesty about the concept's incompleteness is preserved in this registration.
- **The www.motuper.com domain.** The domain that the registrant purchased and continues to own. The domain is the concrete artefact that distinguishes MOTUPER from a name that exists only in private thinking.
- **TRUEDREAM** (www.truedream.org). Another domain owned by the registrant that exists in a similar status — reserved for a concept that has accumulated only a name and a small associative weight. The two domains together represent the registrant's small collection of reserved intellectual territory that has not been developed into specific products.

The specific contribution of MOTUPER is the name itself, the domain registration, and the gesture toward a hybrid computing architecture, with the honest acknowledgement that the substantive concept has not been articulated and may never be.

## Remarks

This registration is unusual within ideaREG in that it captures an idea at a stage where most registries would consider it insufficient for registration. The honest framing of what MOTUPER currently is — a name, a domain, a vague gesture, an admitted possibility that the inventor may never invent — is the registration's distinguishing feature.

The Bible's broader framework recognises that ideas exist at many stages of development. Most public registries capture only ideas at the *fully articulated and ready to implement* stage. ideaREG accepts ideas at earlier stages — including the *I have a name but not yet a concept* stage where MOTUPER currently sits.

The registration may be valuable in several specific ways. First, it documents the name's existing public claim, so that any future developer who arrives at the same name independently will discover that the name has been claimed since this registration date. Second, it creates a small piece of associative weight around the name that anyone developing a real MOTUPER architecture in the future can either build on or push against. Third, it provides a stable public reference point that future related proposals can use as their intellectual-property foundation.

The honest acknowledgement that the registrant may never invent MOTUPER is part of the registration's character. The MIT license under which this registration is made means that anyone is free to develop a real MOTUPER concept. If someone else produces the first working motuper, the name is theirs to use.

The registration documents MOTUPER as it actually exists — as a name, as a domain, as a gesture, as a placeholder. Future development, if any occurs, may produce a real architecture. Or the name may remain a placeholder forever. Both outcomes are acceptable. The registration captures the current state honestly.
