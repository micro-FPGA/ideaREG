## Idea

### Title

**Love as Error-Correcting Code** — a conceptual framework where unconditional love functions as forward error correction in human relationships.

### Description

The idea applies the engineering concept of **forward error correction (FEC)** as a metaphor for how unconditional love operates in human relationships.

In digital communications, forward error correction is a class of techniques where the transmitter sends additional redundant information alongside the data, allowing the receiver to detect and correct errors without needing to request retransmission. FEC works because the redundancy is general rather than specific — it does not need to predict which errors will occur, only to provide enough redundancy that many possible errors can be absorbed.

The conceptual claim of *Love as Error-Correcting Code* is that unconditional love functions in the same way for human relationships. Misunderstandings between people are like bit flips in a noisy communication channel — small corruptions of intended meaning that occur unavoidably as one person's intentions are transmitted to another. Conventional responses to these corruptions either ignore them (errors accumulate, relationships degrade over time) or attempt to correct them through conflict (the equivalent of requesting retransmission, expensive in relational terms and often unsuccessful because the retransmitted message is itself subject to the same corruption).

Unconditional love provides the redundancy that allows misunderstandings to be absorbed and corrected automatically, without the explicit retransmission step. The underlying commitment carries enough information that individual miscommunications can be reconstructed from context rather than needing to be perfectly resolved at the moment they occur. The result is a relationship that becomes more resilient as the redundancy accumulates, not less, even as the absolute number of misunderstandings grows over time.

### Why this idea matters

The framework explains a real observation about long-term loving relationships. People who love each other unconditionally seem to have an unusual capacity to absorb misunderstandings without those misunderstandings damaging the relationship. This is not because they have fewer misunderstandings — they often have more, because they communicate more — but because the misunderstandings do not propagate the way they would in a relationship without the underlying redundancy.

The framework also has practical implications. If unconditional love functions as FEC, then the actions that strengthen a relationship are the actions that increase the redundancy: repeated demonstrated commitment, consistency of behaviour over time, presence through difficult moments, small acts that accumulate over years. The actions that weaken a relationship are the ones that reduce the redundancy or increase the noise: conditional withdrawal, inconsistency, absence during periods of stress, conditions placed on commitment.

The engineering framing makes the idea accessible to analytic and technical readers who might otherwise dismiss softer articulations of similar observations. The same observation expressed as *love endures all things* invites different reception than *love provides forward error correction in the communication channel of a relationship* — both convey something similar but the second is available to readers who value precision.

### Cross-reference to the Antti Bible

The idea is intended as an expansion of the Bible's *Love is the Answer* framework. The Bible has been developing observations about love across many entries — the *Religion in deep time* entry's claim that the divine is the people who love you back, the *10 EUR* entry's closing where the wife tears the loan paper, the LOVE-as-define-1 observation, the *Faith in God* entry's careful framing. *Love as Error-Correcting Code* adds an engineering metaphor that fits this developing framework while introducing a new angle.

The idea could be expanded into a Bible parable that develops the FEC metaphor in narrative form. The parable might describe a relationship across years, showing how specific misunderstandings get absorbed by the underlying redundancy of demonstrated love, while a parallel relationship without that redundancy degrades under similar corruption. The parable would make the abstract framework concrete through specific human detail.

- **Forward error correction in digital communications.** The technical foundation of the metaphor — Hamming codes, Reed-Solomon codes, convolutional codes, LDPC codes, turbo codes, and the broader field of channel coding theory. Claude Shannon's foundational work on information theory provides the underlying framework.
- **The Antti Bible's developing observations on love** (multiple entries across the Bible, particularly *Religion in deep time*, *10 EUR*, *LOVE = #define LOVE 1*, *Faith in God*).
- **Bell hooks's writings on love as practice** — particularly *All About Love*, which frames love as a verb rather than a feeling, with practical implications for how relationships work. The FEC framing complements this practice-oriented view.
- **The mathematical/engineering metaphor tradition** — using engineering concepts to articulate observations about human experience (Norbert Wiener's cybernetics, Gregory Bateson's systems thinking about relationships, various information-theoretic approaches to social science).

The specific contribution of *Love as Error-Correcting Code* is the FEC metaphor applied to unconditional love specifically, with the explicit framing that the redundancy is what makes long-term relationships resilient to inevitable miscommunication.
