## Idea

**Title:** Anny — the AI Nanny

**Description:**

Anny is an AI nanny. The name is the contraction: **A**I **Nanny** → Anny. It
also belongs to the family of A-names.

Anny is not a phone app and not a cloud service. Anny is a dedicated device
that keeps a child company and keeps watch: reads aloud, answers the endless
questions, sings, plays, notices when something is wrong, and calls a human
when a human is needed.

The idea is not "an AI that looks after children." That is obvious, and several
companies are already selling it. The idea is the constraint:

> **Anny does not phone home.**

All inference runs on the device. There is no network connection in the default
configuration. Nothing is recorded and nothing is retained. No account, no
subscription, no telemetry, no model of the child held on anybody's server. The
hardware can be inspected and the software can be read.

Three further design rules follow from the first:

1. **Anny escalates.** Anny is built to fetch a human, not to substitute for
   one. Anything it cannot handle, it hands over immediately and audibly.
2. **Anny says what it is.** Anny never claims to be a person, never claims to
   love the child, and answers honestly when asked what it is.
3. **Anny keeps no secrets from the parents, and asks the child to keep none.**
   A device that invites a child into confidence against the adults in the
   room is the failure mode to design against, not a feature to add.

**Field or domain:** Embedded AI, edge inference, child safety, privacy-first
hardware design.

**Why this idea matters:**

The current default for AI aimed at children is a microphone in a child's
bedroom, connected to a company's servers, paid for with the child's data. That
is the most invasive object it is possible to put in a home, and it is being
normalised because the cloud is the cheap way to build it.

Local inference on edge silicon is now good enough that it does not have to be
built that way. Anny exists to make the argument in hardware: the same function,
on a device that cannot report on the child even if its maker later wants it to.

Not phoning home is not a privacy feature bolted on afterwards. It is the whole
idea, and everything else is implementation detail.

## Remarks

The hard parts, recorded honestly, because a registration that lists only the
upside is worth less later:

**A nanny's real job is judgement in situations nobody anticipated.** That is
precisely what current systems are worst at. Anny can read a story reliably.
Anny cannot be relied on to recognise the thing that has never happened before,
which is the moment a nanny exists for. This is an argument for a narrow,
honest scope and against every temptation to widen it.

**Attachment is not a bug that can be fixed by a disclaimer.** A child spending
hours with an attentive voice will attach to it, whatever the device says about
itself. Design rule 2 reduces the harm; it does not remove it. The mitigation
is a limit on hours, not a better disclaimer.

**The failure mode is not technical.** It is a tired parent discovering that
Anny is good enough to leave the room. Anny should be built so that this is
difficult — sessions that end, presence that has to be re-established by an
adult, no unattended overnight mode.

**Local-only costs money.** Cloud inference is subsidised; on-device inference
is paid for once, in silicon, by the buyer. Anny will be more expensive than
the thing it argues against. That is the commercial problem to solve, and
pretending otherwise would be the fastest way to lose the argument.
